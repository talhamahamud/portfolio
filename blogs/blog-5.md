Sometimes our spare time can affect us more than we realise! As a physics graduate student, I have always been interested in how different things can affect us in terms of our behaviour, focus and productivity. Recently, I have done a small personal experiment to discover how our use of social media during our 'non productive' times affects our focus. This is not an experiment that I have completed in a scientific way, and I am certainly not a medical expert but I want to share my findings from my personal observation. I found the results to be very interesting!
## The Experiment
I have not been a particularly heavy social media user for a long time. Like many, I occasionally use social media when I don't have anything specific to do. An example of this could be if I am waiting for someone, I am sat by myself, I am travelling or if I have a short break between tasks. We will usually feel that opening Facebook or Instagram (or any social media) is completely fine to do. We don't usually feel like we are wasting our time as we aren't doing anything important. However, I decided to try something different! I tried to not use social media during these times. I didn't change how busy I was with my main work, research or anything else. I just stopped using social media during these small breaks. The results were very interesting! I noticed that I was much more focussed throughout the day! I wasn't getting distracted and could concentrate on what was important to me. I also noticed that when I got back to my research it was easier to get back into the flow of what I was doing. However, when I did go back to using social media during these times, I noticed that I wasn't able to focus as well. Even though I wasn't spending much time on it, it still seemed to have an effect.

This got me thinking… why does doing something for a few minutes during my spare time affect my focus throughout the rest of the day?
## The Hidden Effects
I'm unsure exactly what the psychological/neurological reasons are for what I noticed but I have some ideas! Social media constantly exposes us to other peoples lives. 
When opening social media, we rarely have any control over what we are seeing. Within a few minutes we could be looking at someone's academic successes, someone's luxurious lifestyle, someone's career success, someone's interesting philosophical arguments or a completely different lifestyle. On their own these aren't necessarily bad things to look at. They can be inspiring and educational. However, we are constantly exposed to a variety of things. Imagine you are a physics graduate student trying to complete some research. You have a specific research focus, future ambitions and responsibilities. However, during a short break you open social media and are exposed to multiple people talking about completely different careers, lifestyles, successes and ambitions. Without even realising, you may start to compare your situation to theirs. This could cause you to question whether you are heading in the right direction, create new aspirations or consider new opportunities. You could begin to wonder whether you are progressing fast enough, whether you should do something different or whether you are missing out.

These thoughts may not be realistic or relate to your situation but they could take up your head space. Sometimes I wonder if constant exposure to other peoples lives causes an instability in our minds. We start the day with clear goals but constantly seeing other peoples lives could cause us to lose our connection to what is important to us.
## Split Focus
Another reason could be due to split focus. Research (especially within physics) requires you to be focused. Sometimes we need to really think about something for a while to understand its deeper meaning. Whether it be understanding a complex mathematical derivation, creating a theory, analysing results from a simulation or solving a challenging research problem. If we are constantly exposed to quickly changing information we could become used to jumping between different topics, ideas and stimuli. Within a few minutes we could be moving from a scientific discovery, to something entertaining, to someone's personal life, to political discussions and then to some random news. Even though we may feel like we are taking a break we are constantly switching our focus. I think this constant switching between unrelated information make it harder for us to stay engaged with a challenging task. Switching our focus has cognitive costs. Interrupting our work can cause issues when trying to get back into complex tasks.

Maybe it isn't just how long we spend on social media but how often we are interrupting our natural thinking.
## Why Reddit Felt Different to Me
Interestingly, I noticed that Reddit had a different effect on me. Unlike some social media platforms, I found this to be a more useful source. I could have a specific goal in mind and find the discussions I wanted. I could read about peoples experiences and look into specific topics. I wasn't as interested in seeing peoples personal lives or finding random things.
Reddit felt more like a source of information. Whilst I wouldn't say that Reddit is a non-addictive source, it can become a source of endless scrolling. However, I noticed that I was using it with a purpose. This made me realise that maybe the key difference is whether we are intentionally or unintentionally using social media.

There is a big difference between using technology to find information and letting technology decide what we should focus on.
## Previous Experiment
This wasn't the first time I have noticed this.
When I was studying for my A Level I did a similar experiment on myself. I decided to not use any social media for a certain amount of time, I mean I completely stop using social media. This was about four months, and I noticed the change after 1/2 week later. Interestingly I noticed that I became calmer and didn't have as many angry reactions.
Later on I came across a documentary (name "The Social Dilemma") that talked about some of these potential effects. This made me think about my previous experiment and realise that limiting my unnecessary use of social media could have a positive effect on my emotional wellbeing.

Constantly seeing arguments, controversial opinions, negative news, unrealistic expectations and other peoples lives could affect our emotions. Our brains don't necessarily differentiate between what we are experiencing directly and what we are consuming.
## The Importance of Protecting Our Mental Space
One of the most important things I learnt from this experiment was that just because we have spare time doesn't mean we need constant stimulation. Sometimes waiting for someone, travelling, sitting by yourself or doing nothing can be a valuable experience. We don't always need to be constantly filling our time with information. Letting our minds be free could help us process our thoughts, think about what is important to us and keep us more connected to our responsibilities. This can be really important if you are involved in research. Often research requires patience, determination and being able to stay with a challenging problem without an immediate solution. If we are constantly getting distracted with unrelated information we won't be able to gain a deep understanding.

Perhaps we should rethink how we view our spare time. Sometimes we could use this time to take a break, observe, reflect or just let our minds be.
## A Personal Conclusion
I want to highlight that I am not saying everyone should stop using social media completely. There are many positives to using social media. It can make communication easier, give us more information, learning opportunities and help us connect with communities we may never have encountered. For many people social media can be an important tool in their professional, educational and social life. However, my argument is much smaller. I believe we should be more aware of how even our smallest habits can affect our focus, emotional stability and long term responsibilities. We often only consider how much time we spend on social media but we should also consider the indirect effects it has on the rest of our time. Whilst we may only spend 15 minutes scrolling through social media, we can be left with thoughts, comparisons, aspirations and distractions for much longer. This is what made my personal experiment interesting.
I may not have gained extra hours by not using social media during my spare time, but I felt like I was gaining something much more valuable. My thoughts were much more consistent. Perhaps this is what we should be protecting! Our focus is one of the most valuable things we have. What we constantly let into our minds can affect what we think about, what we want, how we feel and ultimately who we become. Whilst we cannot control everything that affects us from the outside we can make informed decisions about what we expose ourselves to. Sometimes the best thing we can do to improve our productivity is not work harder, learn more or consume more. Sometimes we should just put our phones down and let our minds focus on what really matters.

**One last thing to consider…**
>Maybe the real cost of social media isn't just how long we are looking at our screens but how much focus we lose after putting them down.
